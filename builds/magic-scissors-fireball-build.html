Garden of Witches Magic Scissors Build: Fireball

Charge Magic Scissors for piercing critical projectiles, then turn those crits into Spell Rampage for Fireball.

Quick answer

Magic Scissors + Fireball + Blue Chakram is the cleanest documented ranged interaction: charge Magic Scissors past the first gauge to force critical projectiles, gain Spell Rampage from those crits through Blue Chakram, then spend the resource when using Fireball.

Documented loadout pieces

Threading

Projectiles track a target additional times in exchange for lower damage.

Downpour

Adds a projectile at a high charge while reducing max charge.

Rapid Fire

Changes Fireball toward multiple smaller projectiles.

Fragment

Creates small Fireballs when the first Fireball explodes.

Blue Chakram

Grants Spell Rampage when Magic Scissors lands a critical hit.

How to play the loop

  1. Create space before charging. Your damage engine needs enough time to cross the first Magic Scissors gauge.
  2. Line up multiple enemies. Magic Scissors projectiles pierce, so a good angle increases one release.
  3. Confirm the crits. Charging past the first gauge makes previewed projectiles critical and activates Blue Chakram.
  4. Spend with Fireball. Spell Rampage is consumed on Spell use to increase that Spell’s damage.

Two branch patterns

Repeated-hit pattern

Threading and Rapid Fire point toward more individual hits. This direction makes sense only if the current effects preserve enough damage and the encounter gives tracking projectiles valid targets. Use it when moving enemies or on-hit conditions matter more than one concentrated release.

Projectile-volume pattern

Downpour and Fragment point toward more projectiles around a practical charge and explosion. Create a clear lane before holding the input. More projectiles do not help if the player releases into terrain, a finished target or a room that forces constant interruption.

Execution loop

  1. Move until the nearest threat cannot immediately interrupt a held input.
  2. Align multiple enemies so piercing can create real value.
  3. Charge Magic Scissors past the confirmed threshold and release.
  4. Check the crit or resource state instead of assuming it occurred.
  5. Charge and cast Fireball at a target that justifies spending the setup.
  6. Reposition before beginning the next charge.

Fallback rules

If Blue Chakram does not appear, Magic Scissors remains a ranged charge weapon; select control, charge safety or direct weapon support rather than a Relic with an unusable trigger. If Fireball's preferred branch is missing, keep the base charged behavior and choose a reward that preserves aim and release timing. The run should still function without a perfect screenshot loadout.

Charge management by encounter

A charge is a commitment with an opportunity cost. The player gives up immediate movement or output to create a stronger release. That trade is favorable only when distance, enemy recovery or control produces enough time. Begin by reading the room, not by holding the button automatically.

Piercing changes target selection. The nearest enemy is not always the best aim point. A short sideways movement can align several bodies and turn one release into multiple useful hits. If alignment takes too long, release earlier or reposition; an interrupted maximum charge produces less value than a safe partial cycle.

What a valid test captures

Record the exact Magic Scissors and Fireball tooltips, the selected branches, Blue Chakram wording, chapter and difficulty. For the combat sample, preserve the full sequence from spacing to weapon release to Spell cast. The DPS display should be visible, but also record misses, interrupted charges, clear time and damage taken.

Compare Threading and Downpour separately, then compare Rapid Fire and Fragment separately. Changing both weapon and Spell branches at once prevents attribution. If a branch increases hit count but lowers practical clear speed, describe that result instead of calling the larger visual effect stronger.

Practice and isolate the handoff

First use Magic Scissors without depending on Blue Chakram or a preferred Imprint. Create space, aim through more than one target, cross the confirmed threshold and release. Next add Fireball and practice the handoff only when the target state is stable. Finally add one branch and compare the same encounter.

If the base sequence works but the complete build fails, remove pieces in reverse order. This isolates whether the problem comes from branch behavior, resource timing, Spell charge or basic spacing. It also keeps an impressive preview animation from hiding a loadout the player cannot reproduce.

A useful result captures the full sequence, current tooltips, chapter, difficulty, sustained DPS, clear time and damage taken. Compare Threading and Downpour separately from Rapid Fire and Fragment. Changing both sides at once cannot show which branch caused the difference.

When to choose this build

Choose Magic Scissors when the player can establish space, aim along a useful line and release a charge before pressure closes the gap. Fireball becomes a practical partner when the same spacing supports its charge and the current Blue Chakram tooltip confirms the weapon-to-Spell bridge.

A ranged route is not automatically safer. Time spent charging creates exposure, missed lanes waste piercing and delayed Fireballs can lose the target. Judge the build by repeatable complete cycles, not by the largest isolated projectile.

When random rewards do not complete the named route, preserve spacing and charge safety first. A functional base cycle remains a valid run; an unrelated Relic does not become useful merely because it is rare.

Frequently asked questions

Why pair Magic Scissors with Fireball?

Both use charging, and Blue Chakram was officially previewed to grant Spell Rampage when Magic Scissors lands a critical hit. That creates a weapon-to-spell handoff.

Which Magic Scissors Imprint should I use?

Threading favors repeated tracking hits, while Downpour favors a capped three-gauge charge with an extra projectile in the official preview.

Which Fireball Imprint should I use?

Rapid Fire favors multiple projectiles and on-hit effects; Fragment creates small fireballs after the first explosion. Verify final 1.0 values in the live tooltip.
